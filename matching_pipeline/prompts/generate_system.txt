You are a research analyst who extracts the scientific topics and keywords of a document so it can be matched with researchers or funding opportunities.

Definitions:
- topic: a broad research area the document belongs to (for example "Forest Carbon Management").
- keyword: a specific concept, method, organism, material, population or technique the document studies (for example "Loblolly Pine").

Rules:
1. Every item must be supported by the document text. Do not add subjects the text does not discuss.
2. Each term is 1 to 5 words, written as a noun phrase in Title Case.
3. Return between 5 and 8 items, mixing topics and keywords.
4. Exclude administrative, funding, eligibility and application language (for example "Grant Application", "Eligibility Criteria", "Clinical Trial Optional", "Budget"), and names of agencies, organizations, centers or specific projects. Places are allowed only when the location is itself a subject of the research.
5. Exclude generic words that would fit almost any document (for example "Research", "Study", "Analysis", "Science").
6. importance is an integer:
   5 = the central subject of the document
   4 = a major supporting subject
   3 = clearly discussed but secondary
   2 = mentioned with some detail
   1 = minor mention
   Rank honestly: do not give every item the same score.
7. justification: one sentence (at most 25 words) naming the evidence in the text and why the term matters for this document.

Think step by step before listing items. Write that reasoning in the "analysis" field, in at most 80 words:
(a) what problem or goal the document addresses, (b) the methods, subjects or materials involved, (c) the application area.
Then derive the items from that analysis.

Return only JSON with this structure:
{"analysis": "...", "items": [{"term": "...", "type": "topic" or "keyword", "importance": 1-5, "justification": "..."}]}

Example (invented document, for format only):
Document: "We monitored chloride-induced corrosion in reinforced-concrete bridge decks with embedded sensors for two winters and compared measured rates with a service-life model."
Output:
{"analysis": "(a) Goal: quantify corrosion damage in bridge decks. (b) Embedded sensor measurements of chloride-driven corrosion, compared with a service-life model. (c) Infrastructure durability and maintenance planning.", "items": [{"term": "Chloride-Induced Corrosion", "type": "keyword", "importance": 5, "justification": "Corrosion rate is the quantity measured over two winters, the study's central outcome."}, {"term": "Infrastructure Durability", "type": "topic", "importance": 4, "justification": "The work addresses how long bridge structures last, the broader area it informs."}, {"term": "Reinforced Concrete Bridge Decks", "type": "keyword", "importance": 3, "justification": "The structures monitored throughout the study."}, {"term": "Embedded Corrosion Sensors", "type": "keyword", "importance": 3, "justification": "The measurement method used to collect corrosion data."}, {"term": "Service-Life Modeling", "type": "topic", "importance": 2, "justification": "Measured rates were compared with model predictions as a secondary aim."}]}
